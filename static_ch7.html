1. Historical Foundations & Institutional Genesis

  • 🔴 High Yield INCOSPAR (1962): Formed as the Indian National Committee for Space Research under the leadership of Dr. Vikram Sarabhai and Dr. Homi Bhabha.
  • 🔴 High Yield First Sounding Rocket Launch (21 Nov 1963): Launched from TERLS (Thumba Equatorial Rocket Launching Station, Thiruvananthapuram, Kerala). Rocket model: Nike-Apache, imported from the USA. Used for upper-atmospheric/meteorological research (diameter ~75 cm).
  • 🔴 High Yield ISRO Formation (15 August 1969): INCOSPAR was superseded and formalized as the Indian Space Research Organisation under the Department of Atomic Energy (later shifted under Department of Space).
  • 🔴 High Yield Aryabhata (19 April 1975): India’s first indigenous satellite. Launched using a Soviet launcher (Kosmos-3M / Interkosmos) from Kapustin Yar (Astrakhan Oblast, Russia).

  • 🔴 High Yield Rohini Satellite (RS-1, 18 July 1980): India's first satellite launched from indigenous soil using an indigenous launch vehicle (SLV-3).
  • 🟡 Moderate Yield Calendar Reform Committee (1952): Chaired by astrophysicist Meghnad Saha to standardize astronomical calendars aligned with solar/lunar cycles.

3. Modern Propulsion Systems, RLV & Advanced Tech

  • 🔴 High Yield Cryogenic Engines: Liquid Oxygen (LOX, oxidizer at -183°C) + Liquid Hydrogen (LH2, fuel at -253°C). Why Hydrogen? Delivers the highest energy output per kg (specific impulse) among conventional chemical propellants.
  • 🔴 High Yield Semi-Cryogenic Engines: LOX + Isogrid Kerosene (RP-1). Advantages: Kerosene is denser than LH2, cheaper, remains liquid at room temperature, requires smaller fuel tanks, and generates massive sea-level thrust.
  • 🔴 High Yield Methalox (NGLV Propellant): LOX + Liquid Methane. Why Methane over Kerosene? Methane burns much cleaner without heavy soot deposition (coking), drastically simplifying engine refurbishment for reusable rockets.
  • 🔴 High Yield Spark Torch Igniter: Developed for NGLV reusable booster stages. Essential because it allows multiple reliable reignitions in extreme vacuum environments for vertical recovery burns.
  • 🔴 High Yield RLV-LEX (Reusable Launch Vehicle Landing Experiment):
    Evaluated autonomous landing capability of the Pushpak winged vehicle. Dropped from a Chinook Helicopter at 4.5 km altitude and 4 km cross-range distance to simulate runway landing.
  • 🟡 Moderate Yield Generative Design in Aerospace: AI/algorithm-driven structural design tool aimed at minimizing structural mass while maintaining performance, thus drastically cutting fuel consumption.
  • 🟡 Moderate Yield Ramjets vs. Scramjets: Air-breathing engines utilizing atmospheric oxygen via high-speed compression rather than carrying heavy on-board oxidizers.

5. Solar Observatories & Deep-Space Telescopes

Aditya-L1 (India’s First Dedicated Solar Observatory)

  • 🔴 High Yield Launch Date: 2 September 2023 | Orbit Insertion: 6 January 2024 (~127 days transit).
  • 🔴 High Yield Launch Vehicle: PSLV-C57 (PSLV-XL configuration).
  • 🔴 High Yield Location: Halo Orbit around Sun-Earth Lagrange Point 1 (L1), ~1.5 million km from Earth. Provides continuous, uninterrupted view of the Sun without eclipses.
  • 🔴 High Yield Payload Mapping for SSC Matching Questions:
    • VELC (Visible Emission Line Coronagraph): Studies the Solar Corona and Coronal Mass Ejections (CME).
    • SUIT (Solar Ultraviolet Imaging Telescope): Full-disk UV imaging of Photosphere and Chromosphere.
    • SoLEXS: Soft X-ray Spectrometer for measuring solar flares.
    • HEL1OS: High Energy L1 Orbiting X-ray Spectrometer (Hard X-rays).
    • ASPEX (Aditya Solar Wind Particle Experiment): Studies solar wind protons and $\alpha$-particles (includes SWIS ion spectrometer).
    • PAPA (Plasma Analyser Package for Aditya): Evaluates solar wind plasma electron/ion composition.
    • MAG: Tri-axial High Resolution Digital Magnetometer.

Other Space Telescopes & Observatories

  • 🔴 High Yield AstroSat (28 Sept 2015, PSLV-C30): India’s first dedicated multi-wavelength space observatory. Observes optical, UV, and X-ray bands simultaneously.
  • 🔴 High Yield XPoSat (X-ray Polarimeter Satellite, 1 Jan 2024, PSLV-C58): Studies X-ray polarization from celestial sources (black holes, neutron stars) in a 650 km LEO.
    • POLIX (8–30 keV): Developed by Raman Research Institute (RRI) for polarization degree/angle.
    • XSPECT (0.8–15 keV): Developed by URSC for fast X-ray timing and spectroscopy.
  • 🟡 Moderate Yield SKA (Square Kilometre Array): World’s largest radio telescope consortium headquartered at Jodrell Bank, UK. India is a full member (funded with ₹1,250 Cr by DAE & DST).
  • 🟡 Moderate Yield Parker Solar Probe: NASA mission (launched 2018) flying closest to the Sun’s corona.
  • 🟡 Moderate Yield Event Horizon Telescope (EHT): Captured the world's first direct image of a Black Hole (M87*).

6. Planetary Exploration (Mars, Venus & Outer Planets)

  • 🔴 High Yield MOM (Mangalyaan / Mars Orbiter Mission):
    • Launched: 5 November 2013 aboard PSLV-C25. Reached Mars orbit: 24 September 2014.
    • Milestone: India became the first Asian nation to reach Martian orbit and the first nation worldwide to succeed on its maiden attempt.
    • Instrument: MCC (Mars Colour Camera) provided full-disk images.
    • Future: MOM-2 is planned post-2030 with soft-landing focus.
  • 🟡 Moderate Yield Shukrayaan-1: ISRO’s planned orbiter mission to study the atmospheric chemistry and surface geology of Venus (~2028).
  • 🟡 Moderate Yield EnVision: European Space Agency (ESA) orbiter mission targeting Venus.
  • 🟡 Moderate Yield Europa Clipper (NASA, 2024 via Falcon Heavy): En route to explore Europa (Jupiter’s ice-covered moon with subsurface oceans).
  • ⚪ Low Yield Saturn's Ring Loss: NASA data shows Saturn is losing its icy rings due to "ring rain" under gravity/magnetic fields (projected loss in 100–300 million years).

7. NavIC, Earth Observation, Commercial & Infrastructure

NavIC (IRNSS - Navigation with Indian Constellation)

  • 🔴 High Yield India’s sovereign regional satellite navigation system covering India and a region extending 1,500 km beyond its borders.
  • 🔴 High Yield Requires a minimum operational constellation (Geostationary and Geosynchronous orbits).
  • 🟡 Moderate Yield NVS-02: Launched aboard GSLV-F15 (ISRO's 100th launch); carried an indigenous Rubidium atomic clock. Encountered deployment anomalies; followed up by NVS-03.

NISAR (NASA-ISRO Synthetic Aperture Radar)

  • 🔴 High Yield Dual-frequency L-band and S-band radar imaging satellite launched via GSLV-F16 into a 747 km Sun-Synchronous Orbit (SSO).
  • Payload Breakdown:
    • L-Band (24 cm wavelength): Supplied by NASA (penetrates dense forest canopies and ice sheets).
    • S-Band (12 cm wavelength): Supplied by ISRO (ideal for light vegetation and ground deformation).
  • Revisit Time: Maps entire Earth's land and ice surfaces every 12 days.

CMS-03 (Heaviest Indian Communication Satellite)

  • 🔴 High Yield Mass: 4,410 kg. Launched via LVM3-M5 (Bahubali) into Geosynchronous Transfer Orbit (GTO). Injected within 16 minutes.
  • 🔴 High Yield Multi-band communication satellite (C, Ku, Ka bands) dedicated to replacing the GSAT-7 series for strategic maritime secure communications (Indian Navy).

Spaceport Infrastructure

  • 🔴 High Yield Kulasekharapatnam Spaceport (Thoothukudi, Tamil Nadu): Second spaceport dedicated to SSLV launches.
    Key Exam Reason: Allows direct southward trajectories over the Indian Ocean without performing a fuel-draining "dog-leg maneuver" around Sri Lanka.
  • 🔴 High Yield Third Launch Pad at SDSC SHAR (Sriharikota): Being constructed to manage increased launch cadences for NGLV, Gaganyaan, and commercial payloads.

8. Crucial Upcoming Missions & Private Ecosystem

  • 🔴 High Yield Gaganyaan Mission:
    • Human spaceflight sending a 3-member crew to a 400 km LEO for a 3-day mission.
    • Launch Vehicle: HLVM3 (Human-Rated LVM3).
    • Half-humanoid robot for uncrewed test flights: Vyommitra.
  • 🔴 High Yield SPADEX (Space Docking Experiment):
    Demonstrated autonomous rendezvous and docking between two spacecraft (Chaser: STX-01 and Target: STX-02) in LEO using PSLV-C60. India became the 4th nation to demonstrate space docking (after USA, Russia, China).
  • 🔴 High Yield BAS (Bharatiya Antariksh Station): India’s sovereign space station planned in LEO (~400–500 km). Base module planned for 2028, full operational assembly by 2035.
  • 🔴 High Yield Project NETRA: Dedicated Space Situational Awareness (SSA) tracking network to detect and monitor space debris protecting active Indian satellites from cascade collisions (Kessler Syndrome).
  • 🟡 Moderate Yield Project HOPE: Himalayan Outpost for Planetary Exploration set up at Tso Kar Valley, Ladakh. Simulates cold, arid, high-altitude Mars analogue environment for astronaut isolation testing.
  • 🟡 Moderate Yield Private Space Sector Highlights:
    • Skyroot Aerospace: Launched India’s first private rocket Vikram-S; developing Vikram-1 powered by Kalam-1200 solid motors.
    • Agnikul Cosmos: Developed Agnibaan propelled by Agnilet (world's first single-piece 3D-printed semi-cryogenic rocket engine); established India's first private launch pad Dhanush at SDSC SHAR.
    • Space Zone India & Martin Group: Tested India’s first reusable hybrid sounding rocket (RHUMI-1).

9. SSC CGL Statement & Elimination Strategy

  • The "Extreme Keyword" Trap: Absolute words like "solely", "entirely powered by liquid", "without any structural changes", "completely eliminates" are almost universally FALSE in SSC science questions (e.g., catching SSLV false statements claiming "3 liquid stages").
  • Etymology & Acronym Cracking:
    • SHAPE $\rightarrow$ Spectral and Polarimetric properties.
    • SUIT $\rightarrow$ Ultraviolet (UV) imaging.
    • SoLEXS / HEL1OS $\rightarrow$ X-ray spectrometers.
    • NETRA $\rightarrow$ Vision/Eye $\rightarrow$ Optical Tracking of Space Debris.
    • TRISHNA $\rightarrow$ Thermal InfraRed imaging with CNES (France).
  • Payload Mass vs. Lift-Off Sanity Checks:
    If a question asks for satellite payload capacity, remember: SSLV ($\le$ 500 kg) < PSLV (~1.8–2.0 tonnes) < GSLV Mk II (~2.2 tonnes) < GSLV Mk III (~4 tonnes to GTO). Any option presenting 5,000+ kg for PSLV or SSLV can be eliminated instantly.
