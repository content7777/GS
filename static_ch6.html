🥋 Judo 🔥 Repeatedly Stressed (CGL PYQ Hotspot)

Origins, core principles, mat dimensions, scoring hierarchy, belt system, and penalties.

Core Fundamentals & Nomenclature 🔥 High Yield

  • Meaning: Literally translates to "The Gentle Way" (The Gentle Game).
  • Origin: Originated in Japan from traditional Samurai combat tactics.
  • Founder & Modernization: Formalized in 1882 by Jigoro Kano.
  • Player: Termed Judoka.
  • Uniform: Known as Judogi (formal loose jacket + trousers + belt). Contestants wear White or Blue.
  • Calling Order: White-uniformed judoka is ALWAYS called first to the mat, followed by blue.
  • Core Philosophical Principles:
    • Jita-Kyoei: Mutual welfare, benefit, and prosperity.
    • Seiryoku-Zen'yo: Maximum efficiency with minimum effort (utilizing opponent's momentum).

Match Duration & Scoring Hierarchy 🔥 High Yield

  • Match Length: 4 minutes regulation time.
  • Tie-Breaker: If tied at 4 minutes, goes into unlimited Golden Score (Golden Hour); first point scored wins immediately.
Score Type Point Value How to Score / Conditions Match Impact
Ippon (一本) Full Point (1.0) 1. Throwing opponent on their back with force, speed & control.
2. Holding opponent pinned on back for 20 seconds.
3. Opponent submits (tapping out via choke/arm-lock). 		Ends Match Immediately
Waza-ari (技あり) Half Point (0.5) 1. Throw lacking one element of Ippon (less force/partial back).
2. Holding pin for 10 to 19 seconds. 		2 Waza-ari = 1 Ippon (Waza-ari-awasete-ippon → Match Ends).
Yuko (有効) Lesser Score Historically awarded for 5–9 second hold or minor throw. Used in lecture to explain deciding extra points in Golden Score. Cannot end regulation match alone; acts as tiebreaker score.

Penalties & Belt Rankings ⚡ Core Fact

  • Shido (指導): Minor penalty (warning/infraction, e.g., stepping outside border without resistance, forcing opponent out).
  • Hansoku-make (反則負け): Grave rule breach; results in immediate disqualification.
  • Grade Hierarchy:
    • Kyu (Student / Novice): Ranks count downwards from 6th Kyu (White/Beginner) down to 1st Kyu (Brown).
    • Dan (Master / Black Belt): Ranks count upwards from 1st Dan to 10th Dan.
    • 1st to 5th Dan: Solid Black Belt.
    • 6th to 8th Dan: Alternating Red & White Panel Belt.
    • 9th & 10th Dan: Solid Red Belt (Grandmasters; strictly honorary for legendary lifetime contribution/coaching, not active competitors).

  • Linthoi Chanambam: Created history by becoming the 1st ever Indian to win a Gold medal at the World Cadet Judo Championships (2022) (in women's 57kg).

Game Rules, Shot Clocks & Fouls 🔥 Repeated Question

  • Players on Court: 5 players per team on the floor simultaneously.
  • Shot Clock: The team in possession must attempt a shot hitting the rim within 24 seconds (failure = 24-second shot-clock violation).
  • Fouling Out vs Disqualification:
    • Fouled Out: Occurs when a player accumulates 5 personal fouls (FIBA rules) and must leave the game.
    • Disqualifying Foul: Given for severe unsportsmanlike behavior; results in immediate ejection regardless of prior foul count.

Rules of Play & Lobby Activation 🔥 High Yield

  • Team Composition: 12 players total (7 players on court + 5 substitutes).
  • Match Duration: 40 minutes (divided into two halves of 20 minutes each, with a 5-minute half-time break).
  • Raid Duration: Maximum 30 seconds per raid.
  • Cant: The continuous, clear, audible chant of the word "Kabaddi" in one single breath. Failure to chant or taking a breath in opponent's half awards 1 point to defending team and raider is declared out.
  • Raid Turnover Time: Next raid must start within 5 seconds of previous raid completion.
  • Lobby Activation Rule: The 1-meter yellow lobby on each side is out of bounds until a struggle/touch occurs. If a raider or defender steps into lobby before physical contact, they are immediately OUT. Once touched, the lobby is active and can be used for escape/pursuit.
  • Baulk Line Rule: Raider must cross the Baulk line with at least one foot across and trailing foot off the ground during a raid without touches; otherwise declared out (unless bonus line is crossed with touches).
  • Bonus Point Criteria: Available ONLY when defending team has 6 or 7 defenders on the court (i.e., at least 6). Raider must place one foot beyond bonus line while trailing foot is in the air.
  • Do-or-Die Raid: If a team registers two successive empty raids, the 3rd raid is a Do-or-Die Raid (raider must score a touch/bonus point or is automatically out).

Scoring Terms ⚡ Core Fact

Term Exact Condition Points Awarded
Super Raid A raid where raider scores 3 or more points (via touches or touch + bonus). 3+ Points
Super Tackle When 3 or fewer defenders successfully tackle and pin an incoming raider. 2 Points for Defending Team
Lona Awarded when an entire team is put All-Out. 2 Extra Bonus Points (over & above touch points)

  • Asian Games Debut: Men's Kabaddi was inducted as a regular medal event at Beijing 1990. Women's Kabaddi was introduced at Guangzhou 2010.

♟️ Chess 🔥 Tactical & Board Geometry Hotspot

Board layout, piece capabilities, En Passant, Castling criteria, and Asian Games status.

Board Architecture & Piece Movement ⚡ Core Fact

  • Total Squares (Khane): 64 squares (8 × 8 alternating dark and light).
  • Ranks: Horizontal rows of squares (numbered 1 to 8).
  • Files: Vertical columns of squares (lettered a to h).
  • Total Pieces: 32 pieces (16 per player).
    • Pawns (Pyade): 8 per side. Moves forward 1 square; has option to advance 2 squares on initial move. Captures diagonally forward 1 square.
    • Knights (Horses): 2 per side. Moves in an "L-shape" (2 squares in one direction, then 1 perpendicular). Only piece that can jump over others.
    • Bishops (Unt): 2 per side. Moves any number of vacant squares diagonally.
    • Rooks (Hathi): 2 per side. Moves any number of vacant squares horizontally or vertically.
    • Queen (Rani): 1 per side. Combines power of Rook and Bishop (any number of vacant squares straight or diagonal).
    • King (Raja): 1 per side. Moves 1 square in any direction.

Advanced Rules & Crucial Game States 🔥 High Yield SSC PYQs

Concept Rule / Specific Execution Conditions
Pawn Promotion When a pawn reaches the 8th rank (furthest rank), it can be promoted to Queen, Rook, Bishop, or Knight immediately. A player can have multiple Queens on the board.
Castling Special move involving King and Rook simultaneously (King moves 2 squares towards Rook, Rook hops over King).
Strict Preconditions: 1. Neither King nor that specific Rook has moved yet in the game.
2. No intervening pieces between them.
3. King is NOT currently in check.
4. King does not pass through or land on a square under check.
En Passant French for "in passing". Special pawn capture.
If an opponent advances a pawn 2 squares from starting spot and lands directly adjacent (side-by-side) to your pawn, you can capture it diagonally behind as if it had only moved 1 square.
Rule: Must be executed IMMEDIATELY on the very next move; if not taken instantly, the right is lost.
Checkmate King is under attack (in check) and there is no legal move to escape, block, or capture attacking piece. Game over (Loss for checked player).
Stalemate Player whose turn it is has NO legal moves, but their King is NOT in check. Game is declared an immediate DRAW.
Réti Opening A hypermodern chess opening for White (characterized by 1. Nf3 followed by d4 or c4 with kingside fianchetto), popularized by Richard Réti.

  • Commonwealth Games: Chess has NEVER been included in Commonwealth Games.

Scoring Mechanics Relative to "Par" 🔥 High Yield

Par: The predetermined standard number of strokes a scratch golfer is expected to require to complete a hole (typically Par 3, Par 4, or Par 5).

Term Technical Meaning Example on a Par-4 Hole Example on a Par-5 Hole
Ace (Hole-in-One) Ball goes into the hole on the first single stroke from the tee. Completed in 1 stroke Completed in 1 stroke
Albatross (Double Eagle) 3 Strokes Under Par (-3) Completed in 1 stroke (Ace on Par 4) Completed in 2 strokes
Eagle 2 Strokes Under Par (-2) Completed in 2 strokes Completed in 3 strokes
Birdie 1 Stroke Under Par (-1) Completed in 3 strokes Completed in 4 strokes
Par Even / Standard (E) Completed in 4 strokes Completed in 5 strokes
Bogey 1 Stroke Over Par (+1) Completed in 5 strokes Completed in 6 strokes
Double Bogey 2 Strokes Over Par (+2) Completed in 6 strokes Completed in 7 strokes
Triple Bogey 3 Strokes Over Par (+3) Completed in 7 strokes Completed in 8 strokes
⚠️ Key Conceptual Distinction Emphasized by Sir
An Ace (Hole-in-One) is strictly a physical count of 1 stroke regardless of par. An Albatross is strictly a relative metric of -3 strokes. On a Par-4 hole, an Ace IS an Albatross (4 - 3 = 1). But on a Par-5 hole, an Albatross takes 2 strokes (5 - 3 = 2), so it is not an Ace!
